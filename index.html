      Why Most Cold Emails Die in the Inbox

      Your prospect doesn't hate sales reps—they hate homework. Four mechanical mistakes kill outbound reply rates before a buyer ever finishes sentence two.

      01 · Deliverability

      Spam Filters Read Your Copy Like a Bouncer

      Words like "free," "100% guarantee," "act now," and double exclamation marks raise spam scores in Google Workspace and Microsoft 365. Even when you land in the primary tab, robotic openers like "I hope this email finds you well" scream automated sequence.

      02 · Mobile Reality

      The 3-Second iPhone Preview Test

      Over half of US B2B executives triage email on their phone between meetings. If your subject line wraps past 45 characters or your opening block looks like a tax document, their thumb hits Archive before your value prop ever scrolls into view.

      03 · Buyer Psychology

      The "Me, Myself, and I" Ego Trap

      Starting an email with "My name is Alex and we built a cutting-edge platform" forces a busy stranger to care about your org chart. High-converting cold emails flip the pronoun ratio—leading with a specific problem the prospect already feels.

      04 · Call to Action

      Asking for a 15-Minute Call on Touch One

      Dropping a Calendly link in a first cold email is like asking for a second date before saying hello. Low-friction, interest-based questions ("Worth a quick look?" or "Is Q4 churn on your radar?") start real conversations instead of triggering resistance.

      The 5-Point Cold Email Benchmark

      How high-performing US SaaS sales teams separate an 8%+ positive reply rate from the 0.4% spray-and-pray average.

      Check Bad Habit (Delete on Sight) High-Reply Standard
      Word Count 150–300 words explaining company history, awards, and 6 product features. 50–95 words focused on one sharp pain point and one concrete outcome.
      Subject Line 65+ character sales pitch, fake "Re:" thread, or "Quick question!!!" 2–5 plain lowercase words that look like an internal note (under 45 chars).
      Ego Ratio 70%+ "I / We / Our" sentences bragging about your own product. At least 1:1 "You / Your" focus anchored in the buyer's workflow.
      Readability Single 10-line block of text filled with corporate buzzwords and jargon. 1–2 sentence paragraphs at a 6th–8th grade reading level with clean whitespace.
      Call to Action "Do you have 15 minutes Tuesday or Thursday? Here's my calendar link." Soft interest check: "Open to seeing how we cut ramp time for teams like yours?"

      Frequently Asked Questions

      Straight answers on cold outreach benchmarks, spam filters, and privacy.

      What is a good cold email length? +

      The sweet spot for B2B cold outreach is 50 to 95 words. Emails under 125 words fit cleanly on a single mobile screen without scrolling and give the prospect enough context to reply in under 30 seconds.

      Does Cold Email Roaster store or read my emails? +

      No. Everything runs 100% locally in your browser using Vanilla JavaScript. Zero drafts, subject lines, or customer names ever touch a server. If you click "Save to Local History," that snapshot stays strictly inside your own browser's localStorage until you clear it.

      Why do "15-minute call" CTAs hurt cold email reply rates? +

      A 15-minute meeting request with a stranger isn't actually 15 minutes—it's calendar disruption plus the mental dread of sitting through a pitch deck. Switching to an interest-based CTA (asking permission to share a 2-minute teardown or case study) removes friction and doubles reply volume for most SDR teams.

      What is the ideal "I/We" to "You" ratio in B2B outreach? +

      Aim for at least as many prospect-centered pronouns ("you," "your") as self-centered ones ("I," "we," "our," "my"). When more than 60% of your pronouns point back at your own company, the email reads like a press release instead of a relevant note.

      Which words trigger B2B spam filters in Gmail and Outlook? +

      Classic promotional terms like "free," "100%," "guarantee," "act now," "limited time," and "risk-free"—especially paired with ALL CAPS or multiple exclamation marks—push outbound domains toward Spam or Promotions. Even when they pass filters, tired openers like "hope this finds you well" and "just touching base" trigger instant human spam filters.
